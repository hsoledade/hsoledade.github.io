About

I am a master's student at Shanghai Jiao Tong University, advised by Prof. Yue Wu, graduating in March 2027. I work on the security of AI agents that belong to different organizations and interact through the Model Context Protocol (MCP) and Agent2Agent (A2A).

My thesis project, CrossTrust, asks who may write trust state when organizations do not trust each other. It keeps identities, tool hashes, reputation and evidence on a permissioned Byzantine-fault-tolerant ledger. I evaluated it against 18 systems run from their public code and machine-checked its reputation results in Lean 4.

Before SJTU, I studied at ENSTA Paris – IP Paris and at the University of São Paulo. I am from Salvador, Brazil.

I am looking for a Ph.D. position starting in Fall 2027 in systems security and trustworthy AI agents.

Research

CrossTrust: A Blockchain-Based Trust Architecture for Cross-Organization AI Agents over MCP and A2A
Hazael S. A. Jumonji, Yue Wu, Jianan Hong
Manuscript in preparation · 2026

These attacks already happen. Agents pick a look-alike agent over the genuine one; a tool vetted at installation can later be swapped for a poisoned version (a "rug pull"); most reviewers in a public on-chain agent-reputation registry are coordinated fake identities; and an agent can erase its own traces. Existing defenses answer the four questions through one party that every organization must trust, or through a ledger that anyone can write to.

The idea: decentralize who may write trust state, not just where it is stored. Every call carries one signed field that existing MCP and A2A software ignores safely. The receiver checks it against identities, tool hashes, reputation and evidence kept on a permissioned Byzantine-fault-tolerant ledger with one validator per organization. Each kind of record has one owner, and only a quorum of organizations can override it, so no single organization, including the one that deployed the system, can rewrite what belongs to the others.

What it protects against

  • Identity. Forged, redirected, replayed or expired calls; calls from revoked agents; an attacker registering someone else's name; and an operator taking over another organization's agent identity.
  • Tool and agent integrity. A tool or agent swapped by anyone other than its owner, even on first contact. The owner's own changes are announced before they take effect.
  • Reputation. Fake identities and colluding operators. Ratings count per independent organization, so creating more identities does not help an attacker.
  • Evidence. A party that drops or falsifies the record of an interaction, including along chains of agents that delegate to each other.

Assumptions: fewer than one third of the validators misbehave, and no governance change passes without at least one honest organization. Out of scope: harmful content itself, such as a tool that is poisoned from the start or a prompt injection in a tool's output; CrossTrust tells an agent whom it talks to and what code it runs, and complements content filters.

Next directions

  • Accountability for agents. Evidence of what an agent did that holds even if the operator that ran it lies, and that an outside auditor can check.
  • Collusion-resistant reputation. Aggregation rules with proved bounds against Sybils and colluding operators, cheap enough to run on every call.
  • The tool supply chain of LLM agents. Detecting poisoned or silently changed tools across vendors, without one central registry.

Projects

Fast integer-matrix multiplication on Intel AMX

Research internship · LIX, CNRS & École Polytechnique (MAX team) · Summer 2025 · with Joris van der Hoeven

Tensor units built for AI can also speed up classical high-performance and computer-algebra workloads. I wrote INT8 and INT16 matrix-multiplication kernels with Intel AMX for arbitrary sizes, then profiled them and removed five bottlenecks: per-block memory allocation, repeated AMX initialization, interleaved data preparation and compute, only 3 of the 8 tile registers in use, and scalar data repacking (replaced by SIMD).

Result: up to 1,784 GOPS (INT8, 1024×1024 matrices, Intel Xeon Platinum 8480+), with the multiplication itself taking 98% of the cycles on large matrices.

Autonomous race car

ENSTA Paris team · 2024 – 2025 · 2nd place, Paris-Saclay autonomous car race (CoVAPSy)

A small autonomous car built by our team on a Raspberry Pi and an Arduino, driving only from its sensors. It smooths the 360° LiDAR scan, steers toward the farthest free point while checking for corners, slows down when the road ahead is short or the turn is sharp, and reverses on its own when it is blocked. Video of the race (ENS Paris-Saclay).

My part: the simulator, the camera module, a neural network (YOLO) for obstacle and track detection running on the Raspberry Pi, and LiDAR-based collision avoidance.
